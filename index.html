The eye reads like a sky

Your camera watches how your eyelids behave — how long blinks last, how often they come, how low the lids sit between them — and renders the result as a small sky: bright and structured when rested, collapsing toward a dark core when the eyes are running on empty. It all happens in this tab. No video is stored or sent anywhere.

What it measures

PERCLOS
Share of the last minute the eyes were at least 80% closed. The one signal here with real drowsiness-detection research behind it, so it carries most of the score.
Blink duration
Mean length of recent blinks. Alert blinks are quick; they stretch past 300 ms as drowsiness sets in.
Lids at rest
How far the lids sit closed between blinks, relative to your own calibrated open-eye baseline from the first eight seconds.
Blink rate
Blinks per minute. Very low rates go with screen fixation; very high rates with fatigue and irritation.
Gaze jitter
How much the irises drift over two seconds while you look at the screen — a rough stand-in for fixation stability.
Lighting
Average brightness of the frame. Too dark or too backlit is the single biggest cause of a bad reading in an ordinary webcam, so it is called out directly.

Frames are processed on this device by MediaPipe's face landmarker running in your browser. No video is recorded or uploaded; only the numbers above are kept, in this browser's local storage, so the next reading can show what changed. This is an experiment, not a diagnostic instrument — eyelid behaviour is an indirect, noisy proxy for how tired you are, and none of this is medical guidance.
